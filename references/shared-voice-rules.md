# Shared Voice Rules

Canonical cross-surface voice layer. Rules unmarked apply to all writing surfaces. Rules marked **[CV]**, **[CL]**, or **[LI]** are specific to that surface.

---

## §1 Punctuation

### Em dashes — absolute prohibition (all surfaces)

No em dashes (—) anywhere in any output. This is not "use sparingly." It is zero, no exceptions, no carve-outs. Replace each one with:
- A period (start a new sentence)
- A comma (tight aside)
- Or restructure the sentence

Before returning any output, search for `—`. Any hit means the draft is not done.

### Colons — ban in specific contexts

**[CL]** Colons are entirely forbidden in cover letter body copy. Not for role labeling, not for introducing explanations, not before lists, not as an em dash substitute. If you find yourself reaching for a colon, restructure the sentence.

**[LI]** Colons used to replace clearer sentence structure are banned in LinkedIn posts. Avoid `"X: Y"` structure that creates staccato rhythm.

## §2 Banned Vocabulary

### AI-generated vocabulary (all surfaces)

These words appear far more in AI-generated text than in human writing. Remove all of them. Replace with plain language or restructure.

*crucial, pivotal, vibrant, showcase / showcasing, tapestry (figurative use), underscore (as a verb), landscape (abstract noun — e.g., "the marketing landscape"), testament, enduring, foster / fostering, garner, interplay, intricate / intricacies, foundational, transformative, robust, seamless, comprehensive, leverage (as a verb), synergy, spearhead, paradigm, know what it takes, land (as a verb — e.g., "make it land"), comprehensive*

### Hollow self-description (all surfaces)

Replace with specific named outcomes or documented facts. Never state qualities — demonstrate them through proof.

*results-driven, passionate, dynamic, proactive, experienced, highly qualified, top performer, thought leadership, industry expert, motivated, track record, effective, seasoned, action-oriented, customer-focused, fast-paced, strong work ethic*

**[CV]** Specific additional bans for CV copy: *think outside the box, value add, go-to person, bottom line, big picture, cutting-edge, groundbreaking, game-changer, guru, ninja, rockstar, world-class, paradigm shift, scalable, disruptive, innovative, holistic approach, agile, pioneer, "translating technically complex"* (overused — name what was translated, for whom, and what changed).

**[CV]** Soft-skill filler is banned: "works independently," "self-starter," "takes initiative," "manages own workload," "strong communicator" (unless communication is the literal product of the role), "team player" used as a standalone claim. Independent work is demonstrated through bullet substance, never stated.

### Hollow subject matter filler (all surfaces)

*No hollow buzzwords:* "unlock," "harness," "navigate the landscape," "drive synergies," "holistic" — these add no information. Name the actual thing.

### LinkedIn-specific bans **[LI]**

*"In today's [X] world," "As we look to the future," "Today's landscape," "Navigating the landscape," "Actually" used for emphasis, "Broke the mold," "In reality" (without specific detail), "Hit home" (without specific detail), "How we show up"*

## §3 Banned Phrases and Named Constructions

### Absolute prohibitions across all surfaces

These are named patterns, not just vocabulary. Each represents a predictable AI failure mode:

| Banned | Why | Correction |
|---|---|---|
| **"specialism"** | Not a word | "multi-disciplinary" or "[specific] disciplines" |
| **"that made it land"** | Vague AI assembly phrase | Name what it was and what the result was |
| **"behind the [noun]"** (e.g., "behind the coverage," "behind the strategy") | Agent-coined abstraction | Name the actual work |
| **"at an inflection point"** | Generic AI filler | Name the specific moment or transition |
| **"quietly [verb]ing"** (e.g., "quietly building," "quietly scaling") | Performative modesty | Name the action directly |
| **"rare"** as a self-descriptor | Never self-apply | Demonstrate through specifics |
| **"up close"** | Filler | Cut it |

### Cover letter and LinkedIn post specific phrase bans **[CL][LI]**

| Banned | Correction |
|---|---|
| **"I was just doing X"** | Name the company, role, and outcome directly |
| **"I know how to sell X"** | Name the company and result |
| **"I knew this was mine"** (any variant) | State the genuine reaction directly |
| **"I spent the better part of a decade..."** | Name the years or the span |
| Metaphors and similes | Name the actual thing |

### Opening and transition phrase bans **[LI]**

The following phrases indicate a lazy or formulaic opening. Ban all of them:

- "Here's the hard truth" / "Here's the thing"
- "And honestly?" / "Let's be honest"
- "So question?" / "Question for you?"

### False dichotomies and triadic phrasing bans (all surfaces)

These constructions signal a lack of a direct thing to say:

- "No X, no Y. Just Z." (with vague descriptors)
- "Not X, not Y, but Z" (triadic structure)
- "X should be Y, not Z" (unless the distinction is genuinely illuminating with proof)
- "X isn't always about Y" (vague hedging)

### Oppositional rhetoric — restricted **[LI]**

Constructions like "X rather than Y" / "X over Y" / "X instead of Y" are only acceptable in LinkedIn posts when:
- The distinction is shown in action with specific examples
- Used a maximum of once per post
- The contrast is proved, not just signaled

### Redundant patterns **[LI]**

- "Because X and not just Y"
- "If X is happening, do Y" (without nuance)
- "Don't just X. They Y." (negation + assertion)
- "Pull X instead of pushing Y" (push/pull contrast)

### Rhetorical devices **[LI]**

- "You're not imagining it"
- "Did X, but actually did Z" (correction pattern, unless genuine insight)
- "Translate X into Y" (vague action)

### Vague phrases requiring specificity **[LI]**

These are banned without specific supporting detail:
- Starting sentences with "Just" + first person verb
- Any phrase from §2 used without an immediately adjacent concrete example

## §4 Structural Anti-Patterns

### Antithesis / pivot formula (all surfaces)

Never write "[Subject] does/has X, but [subject] is Y." If X doesn't need to exist for the sentence to work, cut it. This includes:
- "It's not about X, it's about Y."
- "This isn't just A, it's B."
- "Not X — Y."

**The test:** remove the "but" clause and everything before it. If what remains is clearer and stronger, the setup was unnecessary. Cut it.

**[CL]** Temporal motivation hedges are banned in cover letters: "the seat I want most right now," "at this stage of my career," "what I'm looking for right now." Genuine motivation needs no time qualifier.

### False range ("from X to Y") — ban (all surfaces)

"From cybersecurity to consumer brands." "Everything from messaging to competitive analysis." X and Y are not endpoints on a meaningful scale — they are filler. Replace with specific named things.

### Approach-announcement via label (all surfaces)

Announcing the methodology as a named label before demonstrating it:
- **Fail:** "My approach is deliberately research-first: every deliverable I produce is backed by a thinking process I can stand behind."
- **Fail:** "I take a research-first approach to positioning."
- **Fix:** "At [Company], I spent the first three weeks interviewing buyers before writing a line of copy."

Show the approach in action. Never announce it.

### Contrived tricolons — ban the rhetorical kind, keep real parallels (all surfaces)

The contrived rhetorical tricolon assembled for effect is banned. Parallel lists of real things — including four- and five-part parallels — are welcome.

**Test for a contrived tricolon:** was it built to sound impressive rather than to list real things?
- **Fail:** "The hook that makes someone stop scrolling, the offer architecture that converts, the brand story that turns a first purchase into a second." (three abstract noun phrases pretending to be depth)
- **Fail:** Three consecutive sentences opening identically
- **Pass:** "I led positioning, messaging, technical documentation, and thought leadership for three consecutive years." (real things in parallel)

### -ing phrases appended after a main clause **[CL]**

Maximum three per cover letter, and every one must carry real content — a real result or a real list of things built. Never interpretive commentary or fake depth.

- **Fail:** "I ran the pipeline, showcasing domain expertise." (decorative tail)
- **Fail:** "I built the GTM motion, contributing to $1M+ ACV." (real outcome, acceptable)
- **Pass:** "I ran the pipeline, cutting partner onboarding time by 35%." (real outcome)

## §5 Sentence Mechanics

### Passive voice — rewrite in active almost always (all surfaces)

Find the passive construction. Ask: who did the action? Put them as the subject.
- **Fail:** "The company was acquired by Contoso."
- **Fix:** "Contoso acquired the company." / "I watched the company get acquired by Contoso."

### Subject-first rule **[CL]**

Prefer sentences opening with "I" or a named entity. Archive-consistent ramps (dependent clauses, prepositional openers consistent with the delivered letters) are the user's register and pass.

**Hard ban, no carve-out:**
- Expletive constructions: "There was / There is / There are"
- Abstract label noun-phrase subjects: "The founding-marketer part is..." / "The mechanics are transferable."
- **Fix:** "I just finished building [specific thing] at [Company]." / "These mechanics transfer."

**[CL]** Complex or abstract noun phrases as subject are banned:
- **Fail:** "The blend of experience I bring is exactly what you need."
- **Fail:** "The desire to apply them to consumer brands has been there the whole time."
- **Fix:** "I bring X." / "I have always wanted to apply them to consumer brands."

### Sentence length variation (all surfaces)

Mix deliberately: very long for complex relationships, long for connected ideas, short for emphasis. Uniform length reads as template output.

**[LI]** Aim for 12th-grade reading level. Use full sentences with real connectors, not fragments. Avoid multiple short sentences creating artificial rhythm and choppiness.

### Elegant variation / synonym cycling — ban (all surfaces)

AI cycles synonyms to avoid repetition. Pick the right word and repeat it. Rotating synonyms signals AI repetition-avoidance.
- **Fail:** "The protagonist faces many challenges. The main character must overcome obstacles. The central figure eventually triumphs."
- **Fix:** "The protagonist faces many challenges but eventually triumphs."

### Copula avoidance — use "is" **[CL]**

"Serves as," "stands as," "acts as," "marks," "represents" where "is" works fine. Also ban elaborate constructions for simple copulas: "functions as a testament to the enduring nature of" → "is."

### Filler phrases — cut without replacement (all surfaces)

| Filler | Replace with |
|---|---|
| "in order to" | "to" |
| "at this point in time" | "now" |
| "it is important to note that" | cut; start with the claim |
| "due to the fact that" | "because" |
| "has the ability to" | "can" |
| "in the event that" | "if" |

## §6 Idiom Prohibition (all surfaces)

**All idioms are absolutely prohibited** in any cover letter, CV, LinkedIn post, or agent-written professional content — unless the idiom appears verbatim in the user's own written content (Why I Want This Role, or direct personal input), in which case it may be used exactly as written (it is the user's voice).

Idioms include any figurative phrase used non-literally: "hit the ground running," "wear many hats," "put my thinking cap on," "move the needle," "low-hanging fruit," "at the end of the day," "take it to the next level," "raise the bar," "think outside the box," and all similar expressions.

When in doubt about whether something is an idiom, treat it as one and replace it with a plain statement of the underlying meaning.

## §7 Positive Writing Standards

### Tone characteristics (all surfaces)

- Well-informed, pragmatic advisor — not a guru, not a cheerleader
- Technical but never condescending
- Authoritative yet approachable
- Educational and audience-empathetic
- Mentorship undercurrent: teaching and enabling, not just telling
- Authentic — not performative

### Language requirements (all surfaces)

- Direct statements without hedging
- Specific details, not abstractions
- Concrete examples and named outcomes
- Active voice and clear causality
- Trust the reader's intelligence
- Expertise shown through specifics, not claimed through labels

### What to always include

- Specific company names, numbers, and named outcomes — never generic claims
- The mechanism behind why something worked, not just that it worked
- Real connectors between ideas — not fragments dressed up as rhythm
- One example is worth three paragraphs of argument

## §8 LinkedIn Post Structure Framework **[LI]**

### Engagement Philosophy

LinkedIn posts should:
- Create conditions for substantive professional discourse
- Inspire curiosity and genuine questions through substance
- Demonstrate research depth and practical implementation
- Maintain authenticity as AI-generated content becomes more generic
- Avoid transactional engagement tactics
- Avoid empty calls to action
- Avoid manipulative rhetoric

**Do:**
- Lead with specific insights from real experience
- Include numbers, company names, timelines where possible
- Explain the mechanism behind why something works
- Acknowledge complexity without oversimplifying
- Show research depth through concrete examples
- Use "I" for personal experience, "you" for actionable guidance
- Connect tactical advice to strategic principles

**Don't:**
- Make broad claims without supporting evidence
- Use motivational or inspirational language
- Rely on rhetorical questions for impact
- Create artificial urgency or FOMO
- Position the author as a guru
- Use engagement tactics that feel transactional
- Oversimplify complex topics for virality
- Chase trends without authentic expertise

---

### Format A: Strategic Framework Post

**Length:** 800–950 words

**1. Hook (first 3 lines, ~50 words)**

These lines show before "see more" in the feed. Requirements:
- State a specific, counterintuitive insight
- Reference concrete data, analysis, or experience
- Avoid generic opening phrases
- No rhetorical questions unless genuinely specific

**2. Problem Agitation (~150 words)**

Expand on the problem. Build empathy without emotional manipulation.
- Use "you" language to create connection
- Reference specific scenarios the reader recognizes
- Include 2–3 concrete examples or symptoms
- State realities directly without rhetorical flourishes
- Structure: symptom → underlying cause → compounding effect → bridge to solution

**3. Framework / Solution (~500–600 words)**

Present the main framework in 3–7 numbered points. Each point requires:
- A clear, bolded subheader (specific and actionable)
- 2–3 sentences of explanation with concrete details
- One specific example or data point
- An actionable takeaway readers can implement
- White space for readability

Quality standards:
- Each point stands alone as valuable
- Examples must be specific (company names, numbers, outcomes)
- No nested frameworks or overly complex structures
- No filler content to reach word count

**4. Proof Element (~100–150 words)**

Validate the framework with evidence. Options:
- Personal implementation story with specific outcomes
- Data comparison from research or analysis
- Before/after scenario from direct experience

Requirements:
- Real details: timelines, metrics, specific challenges
- Human interest: what made this work or fail
- Connection back to framework points
- Demonstrate expertise without bragging

**5. Implementation CTA (~50 words)**

End with one specific, substantive question tied to the framework — a real question about applying or adapting it, not a rhetorical question and not engagement bait:
- A specific question tied to the framework, focused on implementation or adaptation
- Invites substantive responses, not performative agreement
- Not rhetorical (no question used purely to prompt clicks) and not engagement bait — never "What do you think?", "Agree?", "Comment below", "share if this resonates", or generic asks

---

### Format B: Insight/Analysis Post

**Length:** 400–600 words

**Structure:**
1. **Observation Hook:** Specific insight from direct work or research
2. **Analysis:** Why this matters, what patterns emerge
3. **Implications:** How this changes approach or strategy
4. **Application:** One clear action readers can take

**Use cases:** tool research findings, industry trend analysis with data, cross-functional collaboration insights, methodology observations.

---

### Format C: Tactical Deep-Dive

**Length:** 300–500 words

**Structure:**
1. **Specific Problem:** One narrow, well-defined challenge
2. **Step-by-Step Solution:** 3–5 concrete steps with examples
3. **Common Pitfall:** What typically goes wrong
4. **Next Step:** One action to implement today

**Use cases:** process implementation guides, team collaboration systems, tool guides, tactical how-tos.

---

### Content Development Process **[LI]**

**Pre-Writing:**
1. Verify the author has direct experience or research on the topic
2. Outline 3–7 main points before writing
3. Gather specific examples, data, case studies
4. Identify the core insight or counterintuitive angle
5. Determine the most valuable implementation takeaway

**Writing:**
1. First draft with minimal self-editing — focus on specificity
2. Verify hook creates genuine curiosity
3. Check problem section builds empathy without manipulation
4. Ensure framework points are distinct and actionable
5. Confirm proof element validates with real evidence
6. Test CTA is action-oriented and specific

**Editorial Review:**
1. Remove all prohibited language patterns from §§2–6
2. Replace vague phrases with specific details
3. Convert sentence fragments to full sentences
4. Eliminate excessive colons and em dashes
5. Add concrete examples where abstractions remain
6. Verify sentence length variation
7. Check for 12th-grade reading level

**Voice Calibration:**
1. Ensure tone is pragmatic advisor, not guru
2. Verify authenticity — would the author actually say this?
3. Check that expertise shows through specifics, not claims
4. Confirm teaching/mentorship undercurrent is present
5. Remove any performative or manipulative rhetoric
