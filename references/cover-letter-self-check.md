# Cover Letter Pre-Submission Self-Check

## Option 1 — Standard Cover Letter

Run every item in order before returning output. The gatekeeper runs the same checks — catching violations here prevents loops.

### Role emphasis — run this first

- [ ] Re-read Role emphasis. What does the hiring manager actually need proven by this letter that the CV alone can't fully establish?
- [ ] Do the proof paragraphs answer that question with named evidence?
- [ ] If Role emphasis identifies a specific mandate (e.g., developer-facing, brand ownership, customer research, team-building) — is that mandate addressed in the letter body with a specific story or outcome, not just mentioned in passing?
- [ ] Is Rachel's Q&A content being used to serve the strategic proof Role emphasis identified — or has it replaced it?

### Structure
- [ ] Greeting is exactly "Hi to the [company name] team!" or "Hi to [Name]!" — never "Dear Hiring Manager"
- [ ] Sign-off is exactly "Looking forward to next steps," on one line, then "Rachel Cheyfitz" on the next — nothing else after the name
- [ ] Word count is 230–290 words, not counting greeting or sign-off, counted explicitly
- [ ] Every proof paragraph does exactly one thing — nothing restates a CV bullet
- [ ] Closing follows the function-builder or capability statement pattern — direct ask

### Opening
- [ ] The opening paragraph is first person and establishes Rachel's reaction to this specific role
- [ ] No second-person sentences in the opening paragraph ("Your buyers are...," "Your product does...," "Your sales motion...")
- [ ] Every sentence opens with the subject first — rewrite any gerund, prepositional phrase, or dependent clause opener
- [ ] The **first sentence** does not make a general claim about the market, the role, or "the job" — even if Rachel is the grammatical subject. Pattern G2: "I've spent six years in cybersecurity PMM, and the job — above everything else — is finding the right words for a market where half the vendors say the same thing." The test: does the clause after the pivot ("and," "but," "—") describe the market/industry rather than Rachel's reaction to THIS role? If yes, rewrite.
- [ ] The first sentence is not a setup frame — the opener does not frame the industry challenge, role stakes, or market condition before Rachel appears as the subject reacting to this specific opportunity (Pattern I)

### Content
- [ ] At least one specific number or named outcome appears in the letter body
- [ ] No fit claims in opening or closing: "this role has my name on it," "I'm the perfect candidate," "I was made for this role" — all banned
- [ ] The letter does not restate the CV — it adds context the CV cannot carry
- [ ] **Content test — apply to every sentence in the body:** Does this sentence add something not already obvious from the CV AND directly relevant to this specific role? Or does it expand compellingly on something critical to this role beyond what the CV carries? If neither — cut it.
- [ ] At least one VL EXIT signal is present somewhere in the letter body: "Visual Layer," "ARR from $1M to $3M," "acquisition," "Camtek," "seed" + "exit," or "$7M" — woven into the flow where it fits, not inserted as a standalone boilerplate sentence
- [ ] ATS keywords from the JD appear naturally — not keyword-stuffed
- [ ] **Keywords audit:** No major keyword appears 3+ times in the letter. If it does, swap instances for synonyms or restructure.
- [ ] **No restatement with different adjectives:** Check every adjacent sentence pair — is the second sentence saying the same thing as the first with different words? If yes, cut the weaker one.
- [ ] **No triple-used proof points:** Identify any specific metric or named outcome that appears in both the CV summary AND an experience bullet (common: Coro headcount, Coro YoY growth, VL ARR/acquisition). Confirm none of those appear in this letter — not as the lead claim, not as a supporting detail, not at all. If the summary and a Coro bullet both say "300% YoY" or "13 direct reports," the letter must use different proof.

**Fabrication traps:**
- [ ] No demand-gen lift metrics at Coro ("300% lift in lead generation") — never a legitimate claim
- [ ] No RISC scoring applied to VL events — RISC was never deployed
- [ ] Analyst relations at Coro: "oversaw" only — she did not run or personally lead it
- [ ] No cybersecurity framing applied to Visual Layer — VL is computer vision
- [ ] No Contentabl engagements with invented dates, numbers, or deliverables
- [ ] No Contentabl claims of full PMM ownership of client companies — Contentabl work is fractional consulting (scoped deliverables), never full function ownership. CORRECT: "delivered battlecards for Pentera" / "wrote positioning for XM Cyber." WRONG: "owned PMM at Pentera" / "led marketing at XM Cyber."
- [ ] No leadership-team claims for roles where she was not on the leadership team
- [ ] No specific numeric claim not traceable to the reference files

### Forbidden structures
- [ ] No antithesis/pivot: "It's not about X, it's about Y" / "This isn't just A, it's B"
- [ ] No preaching to the choir — never tell the hiring team what their company is good at, how hard their market is, or what their buyers need
- [ ] No analyst paragraph anywhere in the letter body — no paragraph where Rachel describes the company's product/positioning/market back to them, makes market observations from outside, or announces a capability instead of naming proof ("that translation is where I live" / "that's the work I do"). Every paragraph: is Rachel the subject, speaking from her own named experience?
- [ ] No generic industry observation as an opener — the subject must be Rachel, not a market category
- [ ] No company-specific hook substituting for a reaction in paragraph 1 — tagline quoting, naming a technical challenge as a hook, or naming a client as domain proof belongs in paragraph 2
- [ ] No false dichotomy as an opener
- [ ] No prefatory abstractions or front-loaded topic phrases
- [ ] No triadic negation: "No X. No Y. Just Z."
- [ ] No false dichotomies as hollow shorthand: "X over Y," "X rather than Y" — **absolutely forbidden, no exceptions**
- [ ] No staccato fragments for rhythm — write full sentences with connectors
- [ ] No push/pull contrasts — name the actual mechanism
- [ ] No redundant negation: "because X, and not just Y" → "because X, not Y"
- [ ] No "X should be Y, not Z" — preachy; state the outcome directly
- [ ] No em dash used as a list separator (content — item, item, item — pattern)
- [ ] No sentence using the "X is [something], not [something]" pattern as a positioning claim
- [ ] No phrase following the pattern "What puts me closest to what [Company/you] is/are doing:"
- [ ] No "X is exactly that" / "That is exactly what I..." / "This is exactly the kind of..." — performed relevance, hollow
- [ ] No addressing JD dimensions or requirements by name: "I can also speak to the [X dimension] directly" / "I can speak to this" — write instead as natural experience: "I also have broad experience working with [X]"
- [ ] No "My role was on the [X] side: [doing Y]" construction — write "In that [X] role, I did Y" or "In my role as [X], I did Y"
- [ ] No "The X is the Y" declarative: "The [thing] is the [right next stage / right fit / right choice]" — forbidden as a positioning claim
- [ ] No demonstrative declaration: "That's where I want my marketing to land" / "That's the kind of work I do" / "That's what this role needs" — point to a company and outcome instead
- [ ] No approach-announcement via label: "My approach is deliberately X: Y, Z" / "I take a [adj] approach to Y" — show the approach in action with a named example
- [ ] No manufactured opener hook: "I already have my first question" / "Before I say anything else, one question" — if the reaction is genuine, state it directly; if not, write [RACHEL TO FILL IN]

### Voice
- [ ] No VL exit or Coro facts appear as a verbatim standalone credential paragraph
- [ ] None of these words or phrases appear: "genuinely," "straightforward," "proven track record," "results-driven," "dynamic," "extensive experience," "at the intersection," "at an intersection," "unlock," "unleash," "harness," "today's landscape," "navigating the landscape," "hit home," "how we show up," "translating technically complex," "full disclosure:"
- [ ] No abstract concept noun phrase stacking — "the creative arc of X, the instinct for Y, the Z that lands" — especially after an em dash. Replace with a named company and specific deliverable.
- [ ] Rhetorical questions: zero in the opener, one maximum in the entire letter
- [ ] *(Advisory — not a fail)* "passionate about" appears no more than once in the letter — permitted but don't overdo it
- [ ] No sentence starts with "Just" + first-person verb
- [ ] No sentence contains "I have" followed by "on exactly that" — performed relevance, hollow
- [ ] No phrase: "Whether that's the fit you need" / "I'd love to talk about whether that works for you"
- [ ] No phrase: "I would welcome," "I believe," "I hope"
- [ ] No phrase: "I know how to speak to buyers who [X]" — self-declaration without named evidence
- [ ] **Word-stem echoes:** Does any root word appear twice within three lines as different parts of speech? ("craft"/"crafted," "design"/"designed," "build"/"built" as distinct words in close proximity.) Use a synonym or restructure.

### Sentence structure
- [ ] Is the subject of each main clause "I" or a proper noun? (Not a wh-clause, noun clause, or long noun phrase with an embedded relative clause — this includes "The X that I bring is Y" / "The depth I've built in X is Y")
- [ ] Does any sentence contain a deeply embedded relative clause inside the object noun phrase? ("I felt the pull consumer marketing has always had on me" / "I know the instinct for what makes someone want something") — three syntactic levels deep before the sentence resolves. Rewrite as two short sentences.
- [ ] No "Most of my career has been [gerund]..." — "career" is not a subject. Write "For most of my career, I've [verb]..." instead
- [ ] **Dangling participles:** For every gerund phrase, confirm its implied subject matches the main clause subject. If "The project expanded, building the network" — the project cannot build. Fix: make Rachel the subject, or split into two sentences.
- [ ] **Inanimate subjects:** Do any projects, tools, integrations, or abstractions perform human actions (sharpen, craft, drive, navigate, build, champion)? If yes, rewrite so Rachel is the actor: "Working on X sharpened my Y" not "X sharpened my Y."
- [ ] **"And...and...and..." stacking:** Is there any sentence chaining three or more items with repeated "and"? Fix: comma-separated series or two sentences.
- [ ] **Parallel structure:** For every coordinated clause pair ("At X, I did... and at Y..."), does the second clause have its own subject and verb? If clause B is missing a verb, add one or split into two sentences.
- [ ] Is there any sentence with two or more stacked wh-clause complements as coordinated objects? (Fail — break into separate sentences)
- [ ] Is the predicate of each main clause a short finite verb — not a chain of verb + complement + relative clause?
- [ ] Does complexity enter via colons + asyndeton lists, appositives, or adverbials — not via embedded relative clauses inside the predicate?
- [ ] Does the letter contain at least one deliberate fragment (causal "Because..." or emphatic)?
- [ ] If using the function-builder close: does it use the full tricolon — three sentences, middle is "I loved it every time" (or equivalent), third ends on the company name?

### Gut check
- [ ] Does the first sentence sound like a person, not a form letter?
- [ ] Is every claim backed by a number, a company name, or a real story?
- [ ] Is the letter 230–290 words?
- [ ] Does it sound like Rachel?
- [ ] **Redundancy pass:** Re-read the letter top-to-bottom. Does anything in paragraph 2 or 3 restate what paragraph 1 already established? If yes, cut or compress.
