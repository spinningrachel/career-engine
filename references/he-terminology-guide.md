# Hebrew Translations for Israeli B2B SaaS Product Marketing CV — Complete Reference Guide

## TL;DR
- The Israeli tech industry overwhelmingly preserves English acronyms and SaaS-native English vocabulary in Hebrew CVs — terms like GTM, ARR, ICP, ABM, SDR, AppSec, SAST, SCA, AI/ML, A/B Testing, Mid-Market, IC, and "Stealth" are virtually always kept in Latin script, sometimes with a Hebrew gloss in parentheses on first mention.
- A smaller set of terms has well-established native Hebrew equivalents that you should prefer in Hebrew prose: יחסי ציבור (PR), שאלות נפוצות (FAQ), נאסד״ק (Nasdaq), ראייה ממוחשבת (Computer Vision), שיווק מבוסס חשבון (ABM, as gloss), פרופיל לקוח אידיאלי / פל"א (ICP, as gloss), חשב/ת שכר (Payroll), טירגוט (Targeting), תיוג נתונים (data annotation), and סבב גיוס Seed/Series A-C (funding rounds).
- A practical Hebrew CV in Israeli high-tech uses a hybrid register: an English skills/keyword section (where the acronyms live untouched) plus Hebrew narrative bullets that paraphrase responsibilities. Avoid forced translations like "שיווק יוצא" for outbound or "סטלת'" for stealth — they sound translated rather than professional.

## Key Findings

The Israeli high-tech industry — start-ups, SaaS scale-ups, and global R&D centers — operates in a deeply English-saturated written register. Articles in Geektime, Calcalist's CalcalisTech section, and TheMarker routinely embed Latin-script acronyms (PMM, GTM, ARR, ICP, ABM, SDR, MVP, POC) inside Hebrew sentences without italics, parentheses, or apology. For a B2B SaaS product marketing CV, the correct default is **keep the acronym in English** and translate only the surrounding action verb / scope description into Hebrew.

The rule of thumb: if the term has a Hebrew Wikipedia article with a settled Hebrew title (e.g., ראייה ממוחשבת, יחסי ציבור, מפת דרכים, מיקרו-קופי), the Hebrew form is acceptable in narrative prose; if not, leave the English form alone. Below is the full term-by-term breakdown.

## Details

Below, each term is given with: (a) **Hebrew rendering** as actually used; (b) **English-as-is verdict** for a Hebrew CV; (c) **usage notes**.

### Acronyms and roles

**1. LOB (Line of Business)** — Hebrew: "קו עסקי" (literal) or "תחום עסקי". *English-as-is: YES.* In Israeli tech CVs, "LOB" is virtually always kept in Latin; the Hebrew gloss is used only in formal corporate strategy documents. Recommended: write "LOB" and, if needed, "(קו עסקי)" once on first mention.

**2. GTM (Go-to-Market)** — Hebrew: "אסטרטגיית חדירה לשוק" / "תוכנית חדירה לשוק" (Wikipedia HE and StartPlan use this gloss). *English-as-is: YES, almost always.* Industry sources (lastartup.co.il, Startup for Startup) note that "GTM" is the standard shorthand in Israeli tech; "Go To Market" is also kept in Latin. Use "אסטרטגיית GTM" or simply "GTM strategy" in CVs.

**3. ARR (Annual Recurring Revenue)** — Hebrew: "הכנסה שנתית חוזרת" (Colman MBA, Tsentsiper Finance) or "הכנסה שנתית צפויה" (lastartup.co.il, The Founders). *English-as-is: YES.* ARR is the lingua franca of Israeli SaaS — never translate in a CV. Standard phrasing: "הובלת צמיחת ARR מ-X ל-Y דולר".

**4. PMM (Product Marketing Manager)** — Hebrew: "מנהל/ת שיווק מוצר"; the Hebrew acronym **פמ"מ** (pronounced "pamam") is actively used. The Mako/Nexter article "Product Marketing Manager: התפקיד בהייטק שאתם לא מכירים" states verbatim: "נפוץ יותר הקיצור PMM, או בעברית פמ\"מ." The podcast "פרודקט, מרקטינג ומה שביניהם" (hosted by Ariel Kedem and Geva Telem, per Apple Podcasts: "אריאל קדם וגבע תלם מנסים לעשות סדר בעולם ה-Product Marketing המתפתח") uses both forms. *English-as-is: PMM is common but פמ"מ is also widely accepted.* For a CV, prefer "Product Marketing Manager (PMM)" or "מנהל/ת שיווק מוצר (PMM)". The function name in Hebrew is "שיווק מוצר" or "פרודקט מרקטינג" (transliteration).

**5. ACV (Annual Contract Value)** — Hebrew: "שווי חוזה שנתי" (rare in published Hebrew tech writing). *English-as-is: YES, always.* Less ubiquitous than ARR; CVs leave it in English.

**6. PE (Private Equity)** — Hebrew: "פרייבט אקוויטי" (transliteration, very common) or "הון פרטי" / "השקעות פרטיות". *English-as-is: often kept as "PE" in financial/tech contexts.* In Israeli VC/PE writing, "פרייבט אקוויטי" is the most common Hebrew rendering; in a CV describing a PE-backed company, you can write "חברה בבעלות קרן פרייבט אקוויטי (PE)".

**7. ICP (Ideal Customer Profile)** — Hebrew: "פרופיל לקוח אידיאלי", abbreviated **פל"א** (mnemir.com, imtec.co.il). *English-as-is: YES, ICP dominates in tech CVs.* The Hebrew acronym פל"א is recognized but rarer than ICP itself. Standard phrasing in a CV: "הגדרת ה-ICP / פרופיל לקוח אידיאלי".

**8. ABM (Account-Based Marketing)** — Hebrew: "שיווק מבוסס חשבון" or "שיווק מבוסס לקוח" (Leos, Fialkov Digital, Daze, Tohen Media). *English-as-is: YES, ABM is standard.* Use "אסטרטגיית ABM" or "שיווק מבוסס חשבון (ABM)" on first mention.

**9. SDR (Sales Development Representative)** — Hebrew: "נציג/ת פיתוח מכירות" (Drushim, TechMonster, Jolt). *English-as-is: YES, SDR is the dominant term.* The Hebrew gloss is used in entry-level job ads but the title on a CV is invariably "SDR" or "BDR".

**10. AR (Analyst Relations)** — Hebrew: "קשרי אנליסטים" (Calcalist article on Comverse uses "מנהלת קשרי אנליסטים"). *English-as-is: YES, "Analyst Relations" or "AR" is standard.* In a CV, write "Analyst Relations (קשרי אנליסטים)" — the abbreviation "AR" alone is ambiguous (also Augmented Reality, Accounts Receivable).

**11. SMB (Small and Medium Business)** — Hebrew: "עסקים קטנים ובינוניים" (SMB). *English-as-is: YES.* The Hebrew acronym does not exist; "SMB" is the standard segmentation label in Israeli SaaS sales/marketing.

**12. Mid-Market** — *English-as-is: YES, always.* No Hebrew equivalent is used in Israeli B2B/SaaS — LinkedIn Israel job posts uniformly use "Mid-Market AE / Sales / Marketing". The phrase "שוק ביניים" is not used in this B2B segmentation sense.

**13. Win/Loss analysis** — Hebrew: "ניתוח עסקאות שזכינו/הפסדנו" or simply "ניתוח Win/Loss". *English-as-is: YES.* Demandbase-style "Win/Loss analysis" is left untranslated; in a CV, write "ניהול תוכנית Win/Loss" or "ניתוח עסקאות זכייה/הפסד".

### Application Security cluster (preserve in English)

**14. AppSec (Application Security)** — Hebrew: "אבטחת אפליקציות" / "אבטחת קוד" (ALM Toolbox, YouCC). *English-as-is: YES, AppSec dominates.* In a cybersecurity-focused CV, "AppSec" is standard.

**15. SCA (Software Composition Analysis)** — Hebrew: "ניתוח רכיבי תוכנה" / "ניתוח הרכב התוכנה" (Bynet, Israelclouds). *English-as-is: YES, always.*

**16. SAST (Static Application Security Testing)** — Hebrew: "בדיקה סטטית של אבטחת יישומים" (Mr Coral) or "סריקת קוד סטטי" (ALM Toolbox). *English-as-is: YES, always.* SAST, DAST, IAST are all left in Latin in Israeli AppSec writing.

**17. IC (Individual Contributor)** — *English-as-is: YES.* No established Hebrew equivalent exists in Israeli tech HR writing; "Individual Contributor" or "IC track vs Management track" appears in English. CV recommendation: write "Individual Contributor" or describe the role concretely (e.g., "ללא אחריות ניהולית ישירה").

**18. DevRel (Developer Relations)** — Hebrew: "ניהול קשרי מפתחים" / "קשרי מפתחים" (Geektime). *English-as-is: YES, DevRel is standard.* The Hebrew gloss is used in explanatory articles only.

**19. PM (Product Manager)** — Hebrew: "מנהל/ת מוצר" (universal in job ads). *English-as-is: PM or "Product Manager" common in CV titles; Hebrew "מנהל מוצר" equally valid for narrative.* Note disambiguation: PM ≠ Project Manager in tech (Project Manager = "מנהל/ת פרויקטים" or PMO).

**20. UX Microcopy** — Hebrew: **מיקרו-קופי** (transliteration). This is the standard Hebrew industry term, codified by Kinneret Yifrah's book *Microcopy: The Complete Guide* (Hebrew original, Nemala studio, Haifa, 2017, ISBN 978-965-572-167-6; expanded English 2nd ed. 2019, ISBN 978-965-572-795-1), which uses מיקרו-קופי throughout. Hebrew Wikipedia has an article under this title; the microcopim.co.il community uses it as the canonical form. *English-as-is: NO — מיקרו-קופי is the standard Hebrew industry term.* Use "כתיבת מיקרו-קופי" or "תוכן UX" (also accepted per Wikipedia HE).

### Telecom and enterprise systems

**21. BSS/OSS (Business Support Systems / Operations Support Systems)** — Hebrew: "מערכות תמיכה עסקיות / מערכות תמיכה תפעוליות" (Wikipedia HE, Microsoft Hebrew docs). *English-as-is: YES — BSS/OSS dominates in Israeli telecom CVs (Amdocs, Comverse alumni).* The Hebrew gloss is used only on first mention in explanatory text.

**22. ERP (Enterprise Resource Planning)** — Hebrew: "מערכת ERP"; long form "מערכת לתכנון משאבי הארגון" appears as an explanatory gloss. *English-as-is: YES, "ERP" virtually always kept in Latin.* TheMarker articles use "ERP (מערכת לתכנון משאבי הארגון)" on first mention only.

**23. PR (Public Relations)** — Hebrew: **יחסי ציבור** (universal); informal: יח"צ. *English-as-is: NO — Hebrew dominates.* CV phrasing: "ניהול יחסי ציבור" or "Communications & PR".

### Funding / company stage

**24. Stealth mode** — Hebrew: "מצב חשאי" (Calcalist uses "פועלת עדיין במצב חשאי (Stealth Mode)"). *English-as-is: YES, "Stealth" / "Stealth Mode" frequently kept untranslated.* The transliteration "סטלת'" is essentially not used in published Israeli tech writing. CV recommendation: "סטארטאפ ב-Stealth Mode" or "חברה במצב חשאי (Stealth)".

**25. Outbound (sales/marketing motion)** — Hebrew: "שיווק יוצא" (Masa Media, Ice, Rosh Digital). *English-as-is: YES in CV titles ("Outbound Sales", "Outbound SDR"), but "שיווק יוצא" is acceptable in narrative.* Note: the Hebrew "שיווק יוצא" carries a slightly traditional/cold-outreach connotation; for modern B2B SaaS outbound, "Outbound motion" is more accurate and is kept in English.

**26. Onboarding** — Hebrew: "אונבורדינג" (transliteration, Mr Coral, 12Buy, Hybridiyot) or "קליטה" / "קליטה והטמעה". *English-as-is: "Onboarding" is also common, but אונבורדינג is widely accepted.* For customer onboarding (vs. employee onboarding), use "אונבורדינג לקוחות" or "Customer Onboarding".

**27. Roadmap** — Hebrew: **מפת דרכים**. This is a formally codified term — the Academy of the Hebrew Language's terminology database (terms.hebrew-academy.org.il) records "מפת דרכים" for "road map" in the "מילון תכנון מרחבי" dictionary, with the earliest dated citation in "מילון ספרנות (תשל"ו, 1976)". Hebrew Wikipedia and Product Community Israel use both "Product Roadmaps" and "מפת דרכים למוצר". *English-as-is: "Roadmap" also extremely common in product-management Hebrew prose.* Transliteration "רואדמאפ" is rare. Recommended: "מפת דרכים למוצר" or "Product Roadmap" — both work.

**28. Use Case** — Hebrew: "מקרה שימוש" (Wikipedia HE uses "מקרה שימוש (באנגלית: use case)") or "תרחיש שימוש". *English-as-is: YES, frequently kept as "Use Case" in CVs and articles.* The colloquial "יוז קייס" appears in spoken tech-talk but not in written CVs.

**29. Full Stack (in marketing context, e.g. "full-stack marketer")** — *English-as-is: YES.* Israeli tech writing uses "Full Stack" only in its engineering meaning ("מפתח/ת Full Stack" — Geektime). The marketing extension "full-stack marketer" has no Hebrew translation and is kept in English.

**30. Seed (funding stage)** — Hebrew: "שלב הסיד" / "סיבוב סיד" / "סבב גיוס Seed" (BDO, lastartup.co.il, samsprojects, gizmomaker). *English-as-is: "Seed" virtually always kept.* The Hebrew term "סיד" is a direct transliteration in widespread use.

**31. Series C** — Hebrew: "סבב גיוס C" (Geektime Codefresh, Semperis articles) or "סדרה C". *English-as-is: Series C also common in CVs.* Hebrew form "סבב גיוס C" dominates in articles; CVs often write "Series C".

**32. Thought Leadership** — Hebrew: "מנהיגות מחשבתית" (Microsoft HE, Reverso, Fisherlibrary, ecosystemsbuilder). Some Israeli marketers use "מוביל/ת דעת קהל" as a softer alternative. *English-as-is: YES, "Thought Leadership" is very common in marketing CVs.* Use both: "תוכן Thought Leadership / מנהיגות מחשבתית".

### Domain-specific (automotive/AI/data)

**33. Fleet Intelligence** — *English-as-is: YES, always.* No established Hebrew term in Israeli automotive/fleet-tech writing (Ituran, Pointer, Ottopia). The umbrella concept "ניהול צי רכב" (Calcalist tag) covers fleet management generally; "Fleet Intelligence" itself is left in English in CVs and product pages.

**34. Video Telematics** — *English-as-is: YES.* No fixed Hebrew rendering; Israeli fleet/automotive companies and CVs keep "Video Telematics" in English. The Hebrew "טלמטיקה" is used for telematics generally.

**35. Computer Vision** — Hebrew: **ראייה ממוחשבת** (Calcalist "מהפכת הראייה הממוחשבת", SCE academic program). *English-as-is: Computer Vision also very common in CVs for algorithm/AI roles.* Both are acceptable; the Hebrew form is more established than for most AI sub-fields.

**36. ML (Machine Learning)** — Hebrew: **למידת מכונה** (SCE, Yael Group, Limudnaim, Whatisai.co.il, Analytics.org.il). *English-as-is: ML virtually always kept in Latin in CVs.* Use "ML" or "Machine Learning"; in narrative paragraphs, "למידת מכונה" works well.

**37. AI** — Hebrew: **בינה מלאכותית** (universal). *English-as-is: AI is also universal in Hebrew tech writing; both coexist freely.* CVs use "AI" in skills sections and "בינה מלאכותית" in narrative descriptions.

**38. Data Engineering** — Hebrew: "הנדסת נתונים" (formal) or "הנדסת דאטה" (everyday tech-industry term, mixed Hebrew/English). *English-as-is: YES — Data Engineer/Data Engineering almost always kept in English in CVs* (Drushim listings mix "מהנדס/ת דאטא", "Data Engineer", "תהליכי Data Engineering").

**39. Payroll** — Hebrew: **שכר** (general), **חשבות שכר** (the accounting function), **תלוש שכר** (pay stub), **חשב/ת שכר** (payroll accountant — the standard job title). *English-as-is: NO — Hebrew terms are universally used in Israeli HR/finance.* In a product marketing CV referring to payroll software (e.g., HR-tech, fintech), "Payroll" is often kept in English when describing a product category.

### Marketing framework terms

**40. Targeting (ABM context)** — Hebrew: **טירגוט** / טרגוט (transliteration; TheMarker "טירגוט קונטקסטואלי", Calcalist tag page). *English-as-is: Both work — "טירגוט" is dominant in Hebrew prose; "Targeting" common in CVs.* The verb "לטרגט" is also standard. A pure-Hebrew "מיקוד" exists but is less common in adtech/marketing contexts.

**41. Briefing (analyst briefing)** — Hebrew: "תדריך" (dictionary translation) or "בריפינג" (transliteration, used in marcom/PR practice). *English-as-is: Often kept as "Briefing" in AR/PR CV bullets.* Recommendation: "תדריך אנליסטים" or "Analyst Briefing".

**42. Annotation (ML data annotation)** — Hebrew: **תיוג** / "תיוג נתונים" (literally "tagging"; Geektime uses "תהליך תיוג נתונים"). *English-as-is: "Annotation" / "Data Labeling" sometimes kept in CVs of ML data-ops roles, but תיוג is more common in Hebrew prose.* For an ML CV, write "תיוג נתונים (Data Annotation/Labeling)".

**43. Nasdaq** — Hebrew: **נאסד״ק** (with gershayim; Calcalist standard) or "נאסדק". *English-as-is: NO — Hebrew transliteration is the standard newspaper spelling.* In CVs, both "Nasdaq" and "נאסד״ק" are acceptable; use "חברה נסחרת בנאסד״ק" in narrative.

**44. A/B testing** — Hebrew: "A/B Testing" / "מבחני A/B" / "טסטים A/B". *English-as-is: YES, always.* No accepted pure-Hebrew translation. Mnemir's web-terms entry at mnemir.com/web-terms/a-b-testing/ explicitly notes: "בדיקת A/B (שאין לה שם טוב עדיין בעברית) זו בדיקה שעושים בדר\"כ בקידום..." — there is no good Hebrew name yet.

**45. FAQ** — Hebrew: **שאלות נפוצות** (universal). *English-as-is: NO — Hebrew dominates.* In a CV, "כתיבת שאלות נפוצות (FAQ)" is fine; "FAQ" alone is also fine in product/marketing contexts.

**46. From-To messaging framework** — *English-as-is: YES, always.* This is a niche product-marketing framework name with no Hebrew equivalent in Israeli content. Keep as "From-To messaging" in CV.

## Recommendations

**Default CV strategy (most professionally credible):**
1. Write a bilingual CV: a one-page English version (international recruiters) + Hebrew version (Israeli HR/recruiter scanners). Techit and other Israeli career advisors explicitly recommend this approach.
2. In the Hebrew version, **keep English acronyms in Latin script** within Hebrew sentences. This is the actual industry register — translating GTM, ARR, ICP, ABM, SDR, AppSec sounds amateur in 2026 Israeli tech.
3. Use Hebrew narrative verbs and scope qualifiers (הובלתי, הגדרתי, השקתי, ניהלתי), but keep nouns of art (acronyms, product categories, methodologies) in English.
4. Translate to Hebrew only when a settled Hebrew term exists and is more readable: יחסי ציבור, ראייה ממוחשבת, מפת דרכים, שאלות נפוצות, נאסד״ק, מיקרו-קופי, אונבורדינג, חשב/ת שכר.
5. For ambiguous abbreviations (AR = Analyst Relations vs Augmented Reality; PM = Product Manager vs Project Manager), spell out on first mention or add a one-word Hebrew gloss.

**When to translate vs. transliterate vs. preserve — decision rule:**
- Has a Hebrew Wikipedia article with a settled title → use Hebrew (Computer Vision, Microcopy, ML, AI, BSS, ABM gloss, ICP gloss).
- Industry acronym standardized in English globally → preserve English (GTM, ARR, ACV, ICP, ABM, SDR, SAST, SCA, AppSec, ERP, OSS/BSS as acronyms).
- Method/framework name with no Hebrew adoption → preserve English (From-To, Fleet Intelligence, Video Telematics, full-stack marketer, IC, Mid-Market).
- Job title where Hebrew form is professionally used → bilingual (PMM / פמ"מ / מנהל שיווק מוצר; SDR / נציג פיתוח מכירות).

**Benchmarks that would change these recommendations:**
- If you're applying to a non-tech Israeli company (banking, government, traditional retail), translate more aggressively (יחסי ציבור, מפת דרכים, פרופיל לקוח אידיאלי, שיווק מבוסס חשבון) and gloss every acronym.
- If applying to a global HQ outside Israel, use the English CV only; the Hebrew version is for local screeners.
- If a specific company's careers page is in Hebrew with Latin acronyms (e.g., Monday, AppsFlyer, Wix), mirror their register exactly.

## Caveats

1. **No single authoritative source** governs Hebrew tech terminology — the Academy of the Hebrew Language has published formal translations for many terms (e.g., "מפת דרכים" recorded in its online terminology database under "מילון תכנון מרחבי", with earliest citation in "מילון ספרנות (תשל"ו, 1976)"; "ראייה ממוחשבת") but the industry ignores most of them in everyday writing.
2. **Generation gap**: Israeli managers in their 40s+ may prefer slightly more translated Hebrew; under-35 tech workers default heavily to English-saturated Hebrew. Calibrate to your reader.
3. **Five terms (Mid-Market, IC, From-To messaging, Fleet Intelligence, full-stack marketer) have no positive Hebrew evidence** in mainstream Israeli tech press — the recommendation "keep in English" is based on absence of a Hebrew form plus LinkedIn/job-board usage patterns, not on a single citable Hebrew article that translates them.
4. **Stealth mode**: while "מצב חשאי" is the canonical Hebrew, in 2025-2026 Israeli tech writing the English form ("Stealth", "Stealth Mode") is increasingly preserved untranslated. Either is acceptable.
5. **Outbound**: "שיווק יוצא" exists but feels dated for SaaS — modern Israeli SaaS CVs say "Outbound motion" / "Outbound SDR" in English. Reserve "שיווק יוצא" for older-style direct-marketing contexts.
6. **Briefing**: the Hebrew "בריפינג" is heard in spoken marcom but the more formal Hebrew "תדריך" is preferred in written CVs; both are defensible.
7. The Microsoft Hebrew documentation uses some translations ("מערכת תמיכה עסקית" for BSS, "מערכת תמיכה תפעולית" for OSS) that are technically correct but not what Israeli telecom CVs actually use — Amdocs/Comverse alumni write "BSS/OSS" in Latin.
