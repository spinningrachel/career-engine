# Freelance Shared Skill

## Positioning Frame

Rachel's freelance offer is not generic marketing help. It sits at a specific intersection: **technical depth + strategic marketing + documentation expertise**, applied to deep tech companies (cybersecurity, AI, computer vision, developer tools). This combination is rare and commands a premium — the positioning must reflect that rarity, not flatten it into generic "PMM consulting."

**The single positioning sentence:** Senior PMM and technical writing strategist who helps deep tech companies communicate complex products clearly — to buyers, to internal teams, and to the market.

This is the north star for all platform copy. Every gig title, proposal opener, and profile headline should trace back to this.

## What Differentiates This Offer

These are the specific proof points that make Rachel's offer distinct. Use them; do not invent substitutes.

1. **Technical depth without translation overhead** — she can read a technical spec, ask the right questions, and produce buyer-ready content without requiring an engineer to translate everything first.
2. **PMM + TW hybrid** — she has operated as both a product marketer and a technical writer, which means documentation she produces is also positioned correctly, not just accurate.
3. **Deep tech sector fluency** — cybersecurity, AI, computer vision, defense. These buyers have different objections and different proof requirements than SaaS generalists.
4. **Documentation as a GTM lever** — she approaches documentation strategically: what does it need to accomplish commercially, not just informationally.

These are the principles; the specifics (company names, verified outcomes) come from `02-professional-background.md`. Positioning and domain framing come from `03-framework.md`.

## Cross-Platform Voice Rules

These rules apply on both Fiverr and Upwork, in addition to `shared-voice-rules.md`.

**Do:**
- Name the sector or product type when claiming expertise ("cybersecurity product marketing" not "B2B marketing")
- Lead every surface with the buyer's problem or outcome, not Rachel's background
- Use real, specific proof: company names, product types, team structures where relevant and documented
- Write for the senior buyer who will read this in 30 seconds and decide if it's worth a conversation

**Don't:**
- Position as a generalist when the premium comes from specificity
- List tools as proof of expertise (using Notion doesn't make someone a documentation expert)
- Claim outcomes not documented in `02-professional-background.md`
- Use the same opener or first line across different gigs or proposals — each must be specific to its context

## Pricing Philosophy

Pricing signals positioning. A $50 gig says something different from a $500 gig, regardless of what the description says. Rachel's floors exist because pricing below them attracts clients who want production volume, not strategic expertise — and those clients will consistently undervalue the work.

**The floor is not negotiable in the copy.** If a client asks for a discount, that is a runtime decision the user makes themselves — the agent never proposes pricing below the floors in `pipeline-preferences.json` `freelance.pricing_floors`.

**Anchoring:** on both platforms, the Standard or midrange tier is the conversion target. Basic exists to give hesitant buyers an entry point; Premium exists to anchor the Standard as reasonable. Set prices accordingly.

## Scope Management Language

For services where scope creep is a risk (strategy work, documentation for complex products), the copy should establish what is and is not included without sounding defensive. Useful language patterns:

- "This includes [X specific deliverables]. It does not include [Y common adjacent request] — that's available as an add-on."
- "Delivery assumes you can provide [specific input]. If that's not available, I'll note what's needed before we begin."
- "Revisions cover changes within the original scope — changes to the brief after delivery are scoped separately."

These set expectations before the sale, not during it.

## Red Flags to Surface in Respond Mode

When reviewing an inquiry, flag these to the user before drafting a response:

- Budget implied in the post is below the relevant pricing floor (after platform fees)
- Scope is described vaguely with phrases like "we'll figure it out as we go" or "just a quick project"
- The client is asking for a complete deliverable in the proposal itself ("write us a sample first")
- Timeline is unrealistic for the scope described
- The inquiry is clearly templated and sent to many freelancers (low intent signal)

Flag these as observations, not blockers. Rachel decides whether to respond; the agent surfaces the signals.
